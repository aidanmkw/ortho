# The Orthodox Apologist — Master Curriculum

> *"Be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you, with meekness and fear."* — 1 Peter 3:15

This document maps every stage, topic, learning objective, and primary source
the game will cover. It is the spine. Q&A items in `/content/stages/...` are
the flesh on this spine.

**Posture.** Eastern Orthodoxy as the Church of the Seven Ecumenical Councils
and the unbroken patristic mind. Cross-examinations engage other traditions
respectfully, accurately, and from their own primary sources — no straw men.
Every Orthodox answer is anchored in (a) Scripture, (b) the Ecumenical
Councils, and/or (c) the Fathers received by the Church.

**Rank progression.**
Inquirer → Catechumen → Reader → Subdeacon → Deacon → Priest →
Archpriest → Confessor → Apologist.

---

## Stage 1 — Early History (AD 33 – 313)
**Rank earned:** Catechumen
**Era:** Pentecost to the Edict of Milan.

### Topics
1. **The Apostolic Era** — Pentecost, Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), the Twelve
   and their sees, the Seventy, the diaspora of the apostles, the
   missionary geography (Andrew to Byzantium/Scythia, Thomas to India,
   Mark to Alexandria, Peter to Antioch then Rome, James the Just in
   Jerusalem, Bartholomew to Armenia).
2. **Apostolic Succession & the Three-Fold Ministry** — bishop, presbyter,
   deacon; the laying on of hands; succession lists in Irenaeus and
   Hegesippus.
3. **The Sub-Apostolic Fathers** — the *Didache*, *1 Clement*, *Epistle of
   Barnabas*, *Shepherd of Hermas*, the seven letters of Ignatius of
   Antioch, *Martyrdom of Polycarp*.
4. **The Ten Persecutions** — Nero (64), Domitian, Trajan, Marcus Aurelius,
   Septimius Severus, Maximinus Thrax, Decius (250), Valerian, Aurelian,
   Diocletian (303–311). Famous martyrs by era.
5. **Early Christian Apologists** — Quadratus, Aristides, Justin Martyr,
   Athenagoras, Theophilus of Antioch, Tatian, Melito of Sardis. The
   pagan polemicists they answered (Celsus, Lucian, Porphyry).
6. **Early Liturgical Patterns** — Pliny's letter to Trajan, Justin's *First
   Apology* 65–67, the *Didache* eucharistic prayers, catacomb iconography,
   Sunday worship from the beginning, fasting from the beginning.
7. **The Edict of Milan (313) & Constantine** — Diocletian's abdication,
   Galerius's edict of toleration (311), the Milvian Bridge (312), the
   end of the Great Persecution.

### Primary sources (read at minimum)
- *Didache*
- *1 Clement to the Corinthians*
- Ignatius of Antioch — seven letters
- Polycarp's *Letter to the Philippians* + *Martyrdom of Polycarp*
- Justin Martyr — *First Apology*, *Dialogue with Trypho*
- Irenaeus of Lyon — *Against Heresies* Bk. III (succession lists)
- Eusebius — *Ecclesiastical History*
- *Acts of the Apostles*

### Sample Q&A types
- Identify which letter contains the line "*Where the bishop is, there let the
  multitude be; just as where Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic
  Church.*" (Ignatius, *Smyrnaeans* 8:2)
- Multiple choice: Which apostle was crucified upside-down, and on whose
  request? (Peter, by his own request — Eusebius *EH* 3.1.2 citing
  Origen)
- Chronology: Order — Nero's persecution, Pliny's letter, Decian persecution,
  Edict of Milan.

## Stage 2 — The Church Fathers
**Rank earned:** Reader
**Era:** ~AD 90 – AD 1359.

### Topics
1. **The Apostolic Fathers** — Clement of Rome, Ignatius of Antioch,
   Polycarp, *Hermas*, *Barnabas*, *Didache*, *Diognetus*, Papias.
2. **The Greek Apologists** — Justin Martyr, Athenagoras, Theophilus,
   Tatian, Aristides, Melito.
3. **The Ante-Nicene Greek Fathers** — Irenaeus, Clement of Alexandria,
   Origen (received with reservation), Hippolytus, Methodius of Olympus,
   Dionysius of Alexandria.
4. **The Ante-Nicene Latin Fathers** — Tertullian (early works only),
   Cyprian of Carthage, Lactantius, Novatian.
5. **The Fourth-Century Greek Fathers** — Athanasius the Great, the three
   Cappadocians (Basil the Great, Gregory the Theologian, Gregory of
   Nyssa), Cyril of Jerusalem, Macarius the Great, Ephrem the Syrian,
   John Chrysostom, Epiphanius of Salamis, Amphilochius of Iconium.
6. **The Latin Fathers as received by Orthodoxy** — Hilary of Poitiers,
   Ambrose of Milan, Jerome, Augustine of Hippo (received with
   reservation on grace/predestination), Leo the Great, Gregory the
   Dialogist.
7. **The Fifth- to Eighth-Century Greek Fathers** — Cyril of Alexandria,
   Theodoret (cautiously), Dionysius the Areopagite (the *Corpus*),
   Maximus the Confessor, John of Damascus, Anastasius of Sinai.
8. **The Byzantine Mystical Tradition** — Symeon the New Theologian,
   Nicetas Stethatos, Gregory of Sinai, Gregory Palamas, Nicholas
   Cabasilas, Mark of Ephesus.
9. **The *Philokalia*** — its compilers (Macarius of Corinth, Nicodemus
   the Hagiorite), its authors from Anthony to Palamas.

### Per-Father drill template
For each major Father, the player must be able to recite:
- dates and see;
- principal works (titles);
- the doctrine he is *most* identified with (Athanasius — homoousios; Basil
  — Holy Spirit; Gregory the Theologian — Trinity; Cyril — hypostatic
  union; Maximus — two wills; John of Damascus — icons; Palamas —
  essence/energies);
- one verbatim quotation;
- the heresy he combated;
- his feast day.

### Sample Q&A types
- Identify-source: "*God became man so that man might become god.*"
  (Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §54.3 — also Irenaeus *AH* V Preface)
- Debate setup: A Reformed friend insists the Fathers were all
  "Protestants before Protestants existed." Cite three statements from
  the first six centuries that disprove this on Eucharist, baptismal
  regeneration, and the perpetual virginity of Mary.

## Stage 3 — Foundational Teaching
**Rank earned:** Subdeacon

### Topics
1. **Holy Trinity** — one essence in three hypostases; the monarchy of the
   Father; eternal generation of the Son; eternal procession of the
   Spirit from the Father; essence vs. energies (Palamas, *Triads*).
2. **Christology** — hypostatic union; *Theotokos*; two natures
   unconfused, unchanged, undivided, inseparable (Chalcedon); two wills
   and two operations (Constantinople III); the *communicatio idiomatum*.
3. **Soteriology** — recapitulation (Irenaeus); ransom & Christus Victor
   (Athanasius, the Cappadocians, Chrysostom); theosis as the *telos*
   (2 Pet 1:4); rejection of strict penal substitution as the controlling
   model.
4. **Pneumatology** — the Spirit's divinity (Basil, *On the Holy Spirit*);
   procession from the Father (Jn 15:26); the Spirit in the sacraments
   and the saints.
5. **Ecclesiology** — One, Holy, Catholic, Apostolic Church (Symbol of
   Faith); conciliarity; the Body of Christ; "outside the Church there is
   no salvation" (Cyprian, *De Unitate* 6).
6. **Sacramentology / The Mysteries** — Baptism, Chrismation, Eucharist,
   Confession, Unction, Marriage, Ordination — and a theology of
   sacrament as participation in uncreated grace, not as juridical sign.
7. **Eschatology** — particular and general judgment; the resurrection
   of the body; the Kingdom; rejection of *apokatastasis* (553) and of
   millenarianism; the question of toll-houses (Seraphim Rose vs.
   critics).
8. **Anthropology** — image (*eikon*) vs. likeness (*homoiosis*);
   ancestral sin not inherited guilt (vs. Augustinian/Western); the
   passions; *nous* and heart.
9. **Mariology** — *Theotokos* (Ephesus 431); *Aeiparthenos*; the
   Dormition; the *Akathist Hymn*; rejection of the Immaculate
   Conception as innovation.

### Primary sources
- Athanasius, *On the Incarnation*
- Basil, *On the Holy Spirit*
- Gregory the Theologian, *Theological Orations* (esp. III–V)
- Cyril of Alexandria, *On the Unity of Christ*
- John of Damascus, *Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*
- Gregory Palamas, *Triads*; *One Hundred and Fifty Chapters*
- Nicholas Cabasilas, *The Life in Christ*; *Commentary on the Divine Liturgy*
- The Symbol of Faith (Niceno-Constantinopolitan) verbatim.

## Stage 4 — Heresies
**Rank earned:** Subdeacon

### Topics (chronological)
1. **Judaizers** (1st c.) — Acts 15; Galatians.
2. **Gnosticism** — Valentinus, Basilides, Marcion; refuted by Irenaeus.
3. **Docetism** — refuted by Ignatius.
4. **Modalism / Sabellianism / Patripassianism** (3rd c.) — refuted by
   Hippolytus, Tertullian (in his orthodox period), Dionysius of Rome
   & Dionysius of Alexandria.
5. **Adoptionism** — Theodotus, Paul of Samosata (deposed at Antioch 268).
6. **Arianism & semi-Arianism** — Arius, Eusebius of Nicomedia, Aetius,
   Eunomius; refuted by Athanasius, Hilary, the Cappadocians; condemned
   at Nicaea 325 and Constantinople 381.
7. **Pneumatomachianism** ("Spirit-fighters", Macedonians) — condemned
   at Constantinople 381.
8. **Apollinarianism** — refuted by Gregory the Theologian
   (*Letter 101 to Cledonius*).
9. **Nestorianism** — refuted by Cyril of Alexandria; condemned at
   Ephesus 431.
10. **Eutychianism / Monophysitism** — condemned at Chalcedon 451.
11. **Monothelitism / Monoenergism** — refuted by Maximus the Confessor;
    condemned at Constantinople III (680–681).
12. **Iconoclasm** — Leo III, Constantine V; refuted by John of Damascus
    & Theodore the Studite; condemned at Nicaea II (787); triumph in
    843 ("Triumph of Orthodoxy").
13. **Pelagianism / Semi-Pelagianism** — refuted by Augustine and
    Cassian (with Orthodox correctives).
14. **Manichaeism, Paulicians, Bogomils, Cathars** — dualisms.
15. **Filioquism** — innovation introduced into Western use; refuted by
    Photios the Great (*Mystagogy of the Holy Spirit*), Mark of Ephesus.
16. **Barlaamism / Anti-Palamism** — refuted at the Hesychast Councils
    (1341, 1347, 1351).
17. **Modern errors (per traditionalist reading)** — Ecumenism as
    "branch theory", Sergianism (subordination of Church to state),
    Name-worshippers (Imiaslavie) controversy.

### Each heresy drill template
- Name and dates of arising.
- Originator(s) and chief proponents.
- Concise heretical proposition (one sentence).
- Why it is heretical (which doctrine it overturns).
- Father(s) who refuted it.
- Council that condemned it (if any).
- One Scripture that disproves it.
- One patristic quotation refuting it.

## Stage 5 — The Ecumenical Councils
**Rank earned:** Deacon

### The Seven (plus key local councils received as ecumenical in force)
1. **Nicaea I (325)** — 318 Fathers; Arius condemned; *homoousios*; first
   part of the Creed; 20 canons; Pascha calculation.
2. **Constantinople I (381)** — 150 Fathers; Pneumatomachi condemned;
   second part of the Creed; canon 3 on Constantinople's primacy.
3. **Ephesus (431)** — Cyril of Alexandria presiding; Nestorius deposed;
   *Theotokos* affirmed; the Twelve Anathemas.
4. **Chalcedon (451)** — 630 Fathers; Eutyches deposed; *Tome of Leo*;
   the Chalcedonian Definition; canon 28.
5. **Constantinople II (553)** — under Justinian; Three Chapters
   condemned; Origen's *apokatastasis* anathematized.
6. **Constantinople III (680–681)** — Monothelitism condemned;
   two wills, two operations; condemnation of Pope Honorius.
7. **Nicaea II (787)** — Irene presiding politically; iconoclasm
   condemned; veneration (*proskynesis*) vs. worship (*latreia*) defined.
8. **The Council in Trullo / Quinisext (692)** — 102 canons; received
   in the East as filling out the disciplinary gaps of V and VI.
9. **Constantinople 879–880** — under Photios; condemned additions to
   the Creed; received by some Orthodox as the Eighth Ecumenical.
10. **The Hesychast Councils (1341, 1347, 1351)** — vindicated Palamas;
    distinction of essence and energies dogmatized.
11. **The Synod of Jerusalem (1672) — Confession of Dositheus** — answers
    to Calvinizing tendencies (Cyril Lucaris controversy).

### For each council
- Convening emperor and presiding Father(s).
- Heresy / question addressed.
- Definitions issued (verbatim where possible).
- Canons of note.
- Reception history.

## Stage 6 — EO vs Roman Catholicism
**Rank earned:** Priest

### Topics
1. **Papal supremacy (universal jurisdiction)** — Vatican I, *Pastor
   Aeternus* (1870); Orthodox response: primacy of honor among equals
   (canon 28 of Chalcedon; canon 3 of Constantinople I).
2. **Papal infallibility** — Vatican I; refuted by case of Pope Honorius
   (condemned by an Ecumenical Council, 681).
3. **The Filioque** — its Latin origins (Toledo councils), its
   universalization at Aachen and at Rome (1014); refuted by Photios's
   *Mystagogy of the Holy Spirit*, Mark of Ephesus's writings against
   Florence.
4. **Azymes** — use of unleavened bread; Cerularius vs. Cardinal
   Humbert (1054); Nicholas Cabasilas's response.
5. **Original sin vs. Ancestral sin** — inheritance of mortality and
   corruptibility, not of guilt; Romans 5:12 in the Greek *eph' hō* vs.
   the Latin *in quo*.
6. **Immaculate Conception (1854)** — Orthodox rejection rooted in (a)
   the absence of Augustinian inherited guilt and (b) the universality
   of the consequences of the Fall (Mary too required salvation by her
   Son, as she herself says in Lk 1:47).
7. **Bodily Assumption dogma (1950)** — Orthodox Dormition vs. Roman
   dogmatic definition.
8. **Purgatory** — refuted by Mark of Ephesus at Florence (1439); the
   condition of departed souls in Orthodox eschatology.
9. **Indulgences and the Treasury of Merits** — late medieval Western
   development; foreign to the patristic mind.
10. **Created vs. uncreated grace** — Palamas vs. Aquinas; the energies
    are God Himself, not a created medium.
11. **Penal substitutionary atonement and juridical soteriology** —
    Anselm's *Cur Deus Homo* contrasted with Athanasius, Irenaeus,
    Cyril, and Chrysostom.
12. **Mandatory celibacy for priests** — vs. Apostolic Canon 5 and the
    Quinisext.
13. **The Petrine texts** — Matt 16:18 (the Rock is the Confession;
    Origen, Chrysostom, Augustine variously); Luke 22:31–32; John 21.
14. **The Council of Florence (1438–9)** — rejected by Orthodoxy; Mark
    of Ephesus's heroic refusal to sign.
15. **Augustine in the East and West** — areas of common confession,
    areas of Western drift.
16. **Modern dialogue** — Balamand 1993, Ravenna 2007 — and their
    limits.

### Debate drill bank (per topic)
Each topic supplies a packet of (a) the RCC claim in its strongest form,
quoting Catholic catechism / *Denzinger* / Council of Trent / Vatican I & II;
(b) the Orthodox rebuttal with patristic, conciliar, and scriptural
citations; (c) anticipated RC rejoinders and replies.

## Stage 7 — EO vs Protestantism
**Rank earned:** Priest

### Track A — General Protestant Doctrines (cross-cutting)
1. **Sola Scriptura** — Scripture *alone* as rule of faith.
   Orthodox reply: 2 Th 2:15; 1 Tim 3:15 ("pillar and ground"); the
   canon itself was given by the Church; *2 Pet 1:20*; Irenaeus
   *Against Heresies* III; Vincent of Lérins's canon.
2. **Sola Fide / Imputed Righteousness** — faith without works.
   Orthodox reply: Jas 2:14–26 verbatim; Mt 25:31–46; Rom 2:6–13;
   Chrysostom's *Homilies on Romans* and *on James*; the entire
   patristic consensus on synergy.
3. **Sola Gratia (as Reformers meant it)** — irresistible grace, monergism.
   Orthodox reply: synergy (1 Cor 3:9; Phil 2:12–13); free will preserved.
4. **Total Depravity** — refuted by patristic anthropology; the *imago
   Dei* is wounded, not erased.
5. **Perspicuity of Scripture** — refuted empirically by 30,000+
   Protestant denominations and theologically by 2 Pet 3:16.
6. **The "Invisible Church"** — refuted by Mt 16:18; 18:17; Eph 4:4–6;
   the visible succession of bishops.
7. **The Canon of Scripture** — the deuterocanonical books as Scripture
   (Septuagint canon, used by the Apostles); rejection of the 16th-c.
   Western pruning. (See also Stage 12.)
8. **Veneration of saints and the *Theotokos*** — Heb 12:1; Rev 5:8; Rev
   6:9–11; intercession of the Church triumphant.
9. **Icons** — Nicaea II; John of Damascus *On the Divine Images*; the
   second commandment in light of Ex 25:18–22 (cherubim made by command).
10. **Sacraments** — Jn 6 (Eucharist); Mt 28:19 + Jn 3:5 (baptism); Jas
    5:14 (unction); Jn 20:23 (confession). Real Presence in all
    pre-Reformation writers.
11. **Liturgy as "man-made tradition"** — refuted by the explicit
    liturgical theology of Justin Martyr, the *Didache*, the
    *Apostolic Tradition* of Hippolytus, the Liturgies of Sts. James
    and Mark.
12. **Apostolic succession** — 1 Tim 4:14; 2 Tim 1:6; Acts 14:23;
    Clement's *1 Clement* on the succession; Irenaeus *AH* III.
13. **Holy Tradition** — its content: Scripture, Creed, Councils,
    Fathers, canons, liturgy, iconography, lives of saints.

## Stage 8 — EO vs Latter-day Saints (Mormonism)
**Rank earned:** Priest

### Topics
1. **The First Vision** — the multiple, divergent accounts (1832, 1835,
   1838, 1842).
2. **The Book of Mormon** — claims and lack of archaeological,
   linguistic, and genetic corroboration; the witnesses and their
   later histories.
3. **The "Great Apostasy"** — the LDS claim that the Church was lost.
   Refuted by Mt 16:18; Mt 28:20; the unbroken historical record from
   the Apostles forward.
4. **Restoration** — Aaronic and Melchizedek priesthoods restored to
   Joseph Smith; refuted by patristic understanding of apostolic
   succession and the Holy Spirit's continuous presence.
5. **The nature of God** — Lorenzo Snow couplet: "As man now is, God
   once was; as God now is, man may become." Compared with the Orthodox
   doctrine of God's *aseity*, infinity, and the Creator/creature
   distinction.
6. **Plurality of Gods** — the King Follett Discourse (1844); refuted
   by Deut 6:4, Is 43:10–11, the Symbol of Faith.
7. **Pre-mortal existence of souls** — refuted by Origenism condemned
   in 553.
8. **Three degrees of glory** — celestial, terrestrial, telestial.
9. **Plural marriage** — the documentary history (D&C 132, polyandry
   with already-married women, the secret affidavits, the 1890
   Manifesto and post-Manifesto polygamy).
10. **Black priesthood ban (1852–1978)** — Brigham Young's teachings,
    the 1978 revelation, the 2013 "Race and the Priesthood" essay.
11. **Theosis vs. exaltation** — surface similarity, radical
    difference. Orthodox: created beings becoming partakers of the
    *uncreated energies* of the one God by grace. LDS: humans become
    *gods* by ontological progression, ruling their own creations.
12. **Baptism for the dead** — 1 Cor 15:29 in Orthodox patristic
    exegesis (Chrysostom *Hom. 40 on 1 Cor*) and in LDS proof.
13. **The Book of Abraham** — the Joseph Smith Papyri recovered
    1966, identified as a standard Egyptian *Book of Breathings* —
    not as Abraham's writings.
14. **DNA evidence and the Lamanite question** — Native American
    origins are East Asian, not Semitic.
15. **Temple ceremonies** — endowment, sealings, garments, the
    pre-1990 changes (penalty oaths removed).
16. **Continuing revelation and prophetic infallibility** —
    contradictions among prophets (Adam-God doctrine; blood atonement;
    racial doctrines).
17. **Engagement strategies** — *meet on documented LDS history* before
    theology; quote *their* sources (Joseph Smith Papers, BYU Studies,
    Gospel Topics Essays). Charity and clarity together.

### Sample debate drill
- *Opponent claim:* "The Book of Mormon's wars and cities are real;
  archaeologists just haven't found them yet."
- *Orthodox response:* Cite the Smithsonian Institution's published
  statement (1996/2007 revisions), the FAIR Mormon admission of the
  geographic problem, and the Jaredite and Nephite metallurgy
  anachronisms (steel, horses, wheels, silk in pre-Columbian America).
  Then pivot from negative critique to the positive claim of the
  historical, archaeologically corroborated Church of the Seven
  Councils — and offer to meet again.

## Stage 9 — Dogma (deep dives)
**Rank earned:** Archpriest

### Topics
1. **Triadology** — *ousia*, *hypostasis*, *physis*, *prosopon*; the
   Cappadocian settlement; essence/energies (Palamas).
2. **Christology** — the Cyrilline–Chalcedonian–Constantinople III
   synthesis; the *enhypostasia* (Leontius of Byzantium); the *agraphos*
   moments.
3. **Pneumatology** — the Spirit's hypostatic property; the
   procession (*ekporeusis*) vs. the temporal mission (*pempsis*).
4. **Soteriology** — recapitulation, ransom, *Christus Victor*, theosis;
   why penal substitution is not the Orthodox controlling model.
5. **Ecclesiology** — *catholicity* (*kath' holon*), conciliarity,
   autocephaly, primacy without supremacy.
6. **Sacramentology** — the seven mysteries; sacramental grace as
   uncreated energy; *opus operatum* vs. participation.
7. **Eschatology** — the parousia; particular vs. general judgment;
   the resurrection of the body; aerial toll-houses (Seraphim Rose vs.
   Lazar Puhalo) — present the *debate*, not a side.
8. **Anthropology** — image vs. likeness; the passions; the *nous*;
   the soul–body composite.
9. **Mariology** — *Theotokos*, *Aeiparthenos*, *Panagia*; rejection of
   the Immaculate Conception; the Dormition (Falling-Asleep).

## Stage 11 — Church Practices
**Rank earned:** Confessor

### Topics
1. **The Divine Liturgy** — anaphora, structure (Liturgy of the
   Catechumens / Liturgy of the Faithful), the three principal liturgies
   (St. John Chrysostom, St. Basil the Great, the Liturgy of the
   Presanctified Gifts); minor and rare (St. James of Jerusalem, St.
   Mark of Alexandria, Western Rite forms).
2. **The Liturgical Year** — the twelve Great Feasts; the moveable
   Paschal cycle; the four fasting seasons (Great Lent, Apostles',
   Dormition, Nativity); the fast-free weeks; Triodion, Pentecostarion,
   Menaion, Octoechos.
3. **The Daily Cycle** — Vespers, Compline, Midnight Office, Matins,
   First Hour, Third, Sixth, Ninth.
4. **Fasting** — Wed/Fri year-round; the four seasons; theology of the
   fast (Basil *Sermon on Fasting*; *Triodion* prayers); fasting in the
   Didache.
5. **Confession** — sacramental confession to a priest; spiritual
   fatherhood; the *Penitential* tradition; non-Latin "satisfactions".
6. **Iconography** — theology (incarnation grounds image; John of
   Damascus, *On the Divine Images*); practice (egg tempera, board
   prep, gilding, the *Hermeneia* of Dionysius of Fourna); rejection of
   "naturalistic" Western art for liturgical use.
7. **Prayer Rule and the Jesus Prayer** — Hesychasm; the *Philokalia*;
   the *Way of a Pilgrim*; the prayer rope (chotki/komboskini); the
   morning and evening rules.
8. **Holy Week** — the Bridegroom services, the Twelve Gospels, the
   Lamentations, Holy Saturday, the Paschal Vigil.
9. **Architecture** — cruciform plan, the dome, narthex/nave/altar, the
   iconostasis (history and theology), the Holy Doors, the Prothesis,
   the Diaconicon.
10. **Vestments** — sticharion, epitrachelion, zone, epimanikia,
    phelonion, omophorion, sakkos, mitre; their symbolic readings in
    Symeon of Thessalonica.
11. **Liturgical Languages** — Koine Greek, Church Slavonic, Romanian,
    Arabic, Georgian, English; the question of vernacular vs. liturgical
    speech.
12. **The Psalter** — divided into twenty *kathismata*; recitation
    cycles; the Psalter in monastic life.
13. **Tonsure** — reader, subdeacon, monastic (rasophore, stavrophore,
    great schema).
14. **Sacramentals** — vigil lamps, incense (kinds and use), holy water
    (Theophany and Lesser), antidoron, prosphora, koliva, the
    panikhida and memorial services.
15. **The Calendar Question** — Julian (Old Calendar) vs. Revised
    Julian (1923); jurisdictions that follow each; relationship to the
    Paschalion (which remains Julian everywhere except Finland).
16. **Akathists, Paraklesis, Canons** — hymnographic forms; the Great
    Canon of St. Andrew of Crete.

## Stage 12 — Scripture (Canon, Hermeneutics, Texts)
**Rank earned:** Confessor

### Topics
1. **The Septuagint** — origin (Letter of Aristeas; the Seventy); use
   by the Apostles (over 80% of NT OT quotations); preferred liturgical
   text of the Church.
2. **The Old Testament canon** — the Orthodox list (39 + the ten or
   so *anaginōskomena*: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach,
   Baruch, Letter of Jeremiah, 1–3 Maccabees, 1 Esdras, additions to
   Esther and Daniel, Psalm 151, Prayer of Manasseh).
3. **The New Testament canon** — the formation; Athanasius's Paschal
   Letter 39 (367); Carthage 397; the Trullan canon 2.
4. **Why the Reformers shortened the OT** — Jerome's preference for
   the *Hebraica veritas*; the role of 16th-c. polemic vs. purgatory;
   Luther's preferred reorganization.
5. **Patristic Hermeneutics** — fourfold sense (literal, allegorical,
   moral, anagogical); Antiochene historical reading; Alexandrian
   allegorical reading; the *consensus patrum*.
6. **Disputed Texts (where the apologist must be sharp)**
   - Matt 16:18 — the Rock.
   - Matt 28:19 — the baptismal formula.
   - John 6 — the Eucharistic discourse.
   - John 14–16 — the Paraclete and the procession.
   - Acts 2 — Pentecost and apostolic preaching.
   - Rom 5:12 — *eph' hō* vs. *in quo*.
   - Rom 9–11 — election and Israel.
   - 1 Cor 11 — the Eucharist; head coverings.
   - 1 Cor 15:29 — baptism for the dead.
   - 2 Cor 4–5 — the body and intermediate state.
   - Eph 2:8–10 — grace and works in their Pauline context.
   - Phil 2 — kenosis; sanctification by fear and trembling.
   - 1 Tim 3:15 — pillar and ground.
   - 2 Tim 3:16 — *theopneustos*; "all scripture" — which canon?
   - Heb 7–10 — single sacrifice and the Eucharist.
   - Jas 2 — faith and works.
   - 1 Pet 3:18–22 — Christ to the spirits; baptism saves.
   - 2 Pet 1:4 — partakers of the divine nature (theosis).
   - 2 Pet 1:20–21 — interpretation of Scripture.
   - 2 Th 2:15 — oral and written tradition.
   - Rev 22:18–19 — adding/subtracting (not a sola Scriptura prooftext).

## Stage 13 — Modern Issues
**Rank earned:** Apologist (in formation)

### Topics
1. **Calendars** — Julian vs. Revised Julian; the 1923 Constantinople
   congress; jurisdictions and the True Orthodox question.
2. **Sergianism** — the 1927 Declaration of Met. Sergius; the Catacomb
   Church; the ROCOR position; reconciliation in 2007.
3. **Ecumenism (per the traditionalist concern)** — the 1965 lifting
   of the 1054 anathemas; WCC participation; Balamand 1993; the
   limits of "ecumenism in time" (St. Justin Popović); Crete 2016.
4. **Jurisdictional plurality in the diaspora** — the canonical
   anomaly; the Assembly of Canonical Orthodox Bishops; the prospects
   for an American autocephaly.
5. **Ukraine and the OCU / UOC-MP** — the 2018 tomos; the canonical
   status disputes; *not* taking partisan sides but knowing the
   arguments.
6. **Bioethics** — abortion (uncompromising opposition; cf. *Didache* 2),
   IVF (problematic for theology of marriage and embryo), end-of-life
   (no active euthanasia, but no obligation to extraordinary means).
7. **Sexuality and Marriage** — the Orthodox dogmatic position; pastoral
   approach.
8. **Genesis and evolution** — the Orthodox latitude (Basil's
   *Hexaemeron*; modern Orthodox voices — Lossky, Florovsky, Hopko,
   Seraphim Rose's *Genesis, Creation and Early Man* as one position
   not the position).
9. **Western Rite Orthodoxy** — its history (Overbeck, the 1870
   project; Antiochian Western Rite Vicariate; ROCOR WR); arguments
   for and against.
10. **Convert pathologies** — hyperdoxy, neo-traditionalism,
    online combox Orthodoxy; what spiritual fathers warn against.

## Stage 14 — Final Trials (capstone)
**Rank earned:** Apologist

A capstone of multi-turn debate scenarios across all stages. Each
scenario:
- presents the conversation partner (an LDS missionary at the door, a
  Reformed Presbyterian in seminary, a Roman Catholic seminarian, an
  Anglo-Catholic, a Pentecostal pastor, a Jewish neighbor, a Muslim
  interlocutor, a secular agnostic);
- gives their opening claim;
- requires the player to construct, turn by turn, the Orthodox
  apologetic, citing Fathers, councils, Scripture, and (where useful)
  the opponent's own confessional documents;
- scores responses against a rubric: accuracy, citation, charity,
  clarity, didactic effectiveness.
